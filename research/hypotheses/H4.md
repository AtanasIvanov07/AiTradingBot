# H4 — Trend filter with volatility-targeted sizing (H1 signal × H3 sizing), BTC/EUR + ETH/EUR

Status: **REGISTERED** (post-hoc, see below). Date drafted: 2026-10-20. Date registered: 2026-10-20 (approved as written).
Shared rules: [_CONVENTIONS.md](_CONVENTIONS.md) (data, timing, costs, metrics, pass rule, variant selection). Unchanged.

> ## ⚠️ Post-hoc hypothesis: informed by the H1–H3 results
>
> Unlike H1–H3, this card was written **after** seeing their Gate 1 results on the same development data (2018–2022),
> journal #5–#7. What was seen and used:
> - **H1** (trend filter, 100% exposure when long) roughly doubled Sharpe vs buy & hold. But drawdowns stayed at
>   53–74% in every variant, because it was fully invested when the fast crashes hit.
> - **H3** (always invested, volatility-scaled) cut the worst drawdown to about −48%. But with average exposure of only
>   about 35–45% and no exit in bear markets, BTC's Sharpe fell below buy & hold, and almost all the return came

## Rule (plain words)

Trade BTC and ETH independently, each in its own sleeve with **50% of capital**. Each day, at the close:

1. **Trend state (H1's rule):** "on" when `close > SMA(N) × (1 + b)`, "off" when `close < SMA(N) × (1 − b)`,
   otherwise unchanged.
2. **Size (H3's rule):** `size = min(1, target_vol / realised_vol)`, with `realised_vol` = std of daily log returns
   over the last L days × √365.
3. **Target exposure** of the sleeve = `size` if the trend is on, else **0** (all EUR).

Orders fill at the next open (conventions §3). While the trend is on, size adjustments only trade if exposure must
change by more than **10 percentage points** (as in H3; also Bitvavo's €5 minimum on a €50 sleeve). Switching the
trend off always exits fully.

## Rationale

- **The standard construction:** time-series momentum as originally published (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, 2012)
  scales each position by its own volatility. H1 left that out, so H4 is closer to the literature's construction
  than H1 was. That's the main non-hindsight argument for it.
- **Mechanism:** the trend filter keeps the strategy out of long declines. Volatility sizing keeps it small in
  violent markets, including the crashes that happen *while* the trend is still on, which is exactly H1's failure
  mode.
- **Who's on the other side:** the same as H1 (trend faders, holders through drawdowns).
- **Honest expectation:** lower returns than H1, since exposure is lower whenever volatility exceeds the target.
  Whether drawdowns get under 40% isn't known, and I deliberately haven't checked. Volatility rises *after* a crash
  starts, so the first leg down is still taken at the old size.

## Parameters (2) and ranges

| Parameter | Values | Count | Where the values come from |
|---|---|---:|---|
| SMA length N (days) | 50, 100, 150, 200 | 4 | Spans H1's range (75 and 125 dropped to fit the cap) |
| Target vol (annualised) | 30%, 40%, 50%, 60% | 4 | H3's full range |

**16 variants** (cap 20). Grid neighbours are adjacent values in either parameter.

**Fixed, not tuned:**
- Band **b = 2%** (middle of H1's {0, 2, 4%}).
- Vol lookback **L = 30 days** (middle of H3's {20, 30, 60}).
- Rebalance band **10 points**.

## Data / period

As H1: primary Kraken BTC/EUR and ETH/EUR, 1d, development 2018-01-01 → 2022-12-31. Confirmation on Binance USDT and
Bitvavo EUR; robustness on Kraken 4h with N × 6 and L × 6.

## Costs

0.30% per side (base), **0.60% (pre-screen)**, 0.90% (informational). Applied to every trade's notional, including
size adjustments.

## Expected trades / year

Trend entries and exits like H1 with b = 2% (roughly 4–10 round trips per asset), **plus** partial size adjustments
while long (roughly 5–20 per asset). Turnover will be reported.

## Success criteria (pre-screen)

**The standard pass rule, conventions §7, unchanged.** This includes **max drawdown ≤ 40%** (criterion 2, as for H1;
H3's relaxed rule doesn't apply, because H4 exits to cash). Criteria apply against each asset's buy & hold and, for
the portfolio, against 50/50 buy & hold.

## Kill criteria

- Fails the conventions §7 pass rule.
- CAGR ≤ 0 at 2× costs on Binance USDT over the same period (as H1).
- Turnover above 400% of capital per year at the selected variant (as H3).

## Max variants

16 (the grid above). Nothing else gets tried under this card.

## Feasibility notes (not part of the test)

- Exposure while long is often 30–60% of a €50 sleeve, i.e. €15–30 positions. 10-point adjustments are €5 trades,
  exactly the Bitvavo minimum.
- Same exchange constraints as H1 (no stop-loss on the exchange through Freqtrade); H4 exits on daily closes.

## Decisions at registration (2026-10-20, approved as written)

1. **Parameters:** the grid spans the same ranges as before, with fixed settings at the middle of the old grids. The
   winners from H1 (n=50/4%) and H3 (30%/60-day) are not reused, because that would be pure hindsight.
2. **Drawdown bar:** the standard ≤ 40%, not H3's relaxed rule.
3. **No "must beat H1" hurdle.** H4 is meant to trade some return for risk, so a variant that meets the 40% limit is
   better than H1 even with a slightly lower Calmar.

## Result / decision

**❌ KILLED at Gate 1 (2026-10-20)**: report [prescreen-H4.md](../reports/prescreen-H4.md), journal #8.

- Selected variant: **n=50, target_vol=60%** (plateau-median score 0.83). At 2× costs:
  - 42.0% CAGR, −46.7% max drawdown, Sharpe 1.08 and Calmar 0.90;
  - against 50/50 buy & hold at 8.5%, −87.0%, 0.51 and 0.10.
- Passed:
  - criteria 1, 3 and 4 for the portfolio, BTC and ETH;
  - criterion 7 (13.9% CAGR without the best year);
  - the Binance kill check (39.3% CAGR on Binance USDT).
- **Failed criterion 2:** drawdown of 46.7% for the portfolio, 54.4% for BTC and 45.9% for ETH, against the 40% limit.
- **Failed the plateau:** both neighbours (n=100/60%, n=50/50%) also break the drawdown limit.
- **Failed the turnover kill:** 940% per year against a 400% cap.
  - Trend entries and exits make up 8.4× of that; volatility resizes add 1.0×.
  - The cap came from H3. It doesn't fit this card's own estimate of 4–10 round trips per asset per year, a drafting
    mistake noted in journal #8. It is not changed after the fact, and it doesn't decide the result: criterion 2 and
    the plateau fail without it.
- Interpretation:
  - Adding volatility sizing to H1 did what the rationale said, in the direction expected: better Sharpe (1.08 vs
    0.99) and a smaller drawdown (−46.7% vs −57.2%).
  - Not by enough at the variant the registered rule selected.
  - The rule selects for Calmar, which favoured the fastest trend and the highest target volatility, the corner where
    drawdowns are largest.
  - Lower-volatility variants that met the limits exist (journal #8), but using them now would mean selecting after
    the fact.
