# H1 — Daily trend following (time-series momentum), BTC/EUR + ETH/EUR

Status: **REGISTERED**. Date drafted: 2026-10-19. Date registered: 2026-10-19 (approved as written).
Shared rules: [_CONVENTIONS.md](_CONVENTIONS.md) (data, timing, costs, metrics, pass rule, variant selection).

## Rule (plain words)

Trade BTC and ETH independently, each in its own sleeve with **50% of capital**.
- **Enter** (go 100% long in that sleeve) when the daily close is above its N-day simple moving average by more than
  a band: `close > SMA(N) × (1 + b)`.
- **Exit** (back to EUR) when the daily close falls below it by more than the band: `close < SMA(N) × (1 − b)`.
- Between the two thresholds, keep whatever position you have. The band adds hysteresis, so a price hovering around the
  average doesn't trigger a trade every few days.

There's no stop-loss and no volatility-based sizing in the pre-screen: this tests the *signal* alone. Sizing
(PROJECT_PLAN.md §6.2) is added in Phase 2 only if the signal passes.

## Rationale (who loses to me, and why)

- **Evidence:** time-series momentum shows up across asset classes over decades (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, 2012),
  and in crypto specifically (Liu & Tsyvinski, 2021).
- **Mechanism:** information gets priced in slowly, investors herd, and capital arrives in waves. In a
  retail-heavy, narrative-driven market like crypto, rallies and crashes tend to persist longer than they "should".
- **Who's on the other side:** traders who fade trends, and holders who ride full drawdowns. Mostly we give up the
  first and last part of every move in exchange for sitting out long declines.
- **Honest expectation:** the benefit is mainly *smaller drawdowns*, not higher returns. In fast V-shaped crashes
  (e.g. 12 March 2020) the filter reacts too late. In sideways markets it loses through whipsaws: repeated small
  entries and exits, each paying costs.

## Data / period

Primary: Kraken BTC/EUR and ETH/EUR, 1d, development 2018-01-01 → 2022-12-31. The longest lookback (200) is
warmed up well before 2018. Confirmation series and the 4h robustness check are as in the conventions (4h: N × 6, same
b values).

## Costs

0.30% per side (base), **0.60% (pre-screen)**, 0.90% (informational).

## Expected trades / year

Roughly **4–12 round trips per asset**: more for short N and no band, fewer for long N and wide bands. At the
0.60% stress cost (1.2% per round trip on a full sleeve), 10 round trips cost about 12% of the sleeve per year.
Costs are a big part of the test.

## Success criteria (pre-screen)

The pass rule in conventions §7, against **each asset's buy & hold**, and for the 50/50 portfolio against
**50/50 buy & hold**.

## Kill criteria

- Fails the conventions §7 pass rule.
- Passes only with b = 0% at N = 50 (the fastest, costliest corner) — that's typically fitting noise.
- Passes on Kraken but **reverses sign** (CAGR ≤ 0 at 2× costs) on Binance USDT over the same period.

## Max variants

18 (the grid above). Nothing else gets tried under this card.
