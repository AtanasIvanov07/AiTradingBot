# H3 — Volatility-targeted holding of BTC and ETH (risk overlay / "smart benchmark")

Status: **REGISTERED**. Date drafted: 2026-10-19. Date registered: 2026-10-19 (approved as written).
Shared rules: [_CONVENTIONS.md](_CONVENTIONS.md) (data, timing, costs, metrics, pass rule, variant selection).

## Rule (plain words)

Always hold BTC and ETH in two **50% sleeves**, but scale each sleeve's exposure down when its market is volatile:

```
exposure = min(1, target_vol / realised_vol)
realised_vol = std of daily log returns over the last L days × √365
```

- Recompute daily at the close. Trade at the next open, but **only when the new exposure differs from the current one
  by more than 10 percentage points** (a fixed band, not tuned). The rest of each sleeve sits in EUR.
- Never more than 100% exposure (no leverage).

## Why this card exists

H3 isn't really an "edge". It answers PROJECT_PLAN.md's question: **"do I even need signals?"**
- If H1 or H2 can't beat H3 risk-adjusted, then their timing adds nothing beyond simply holding less when markets are
  wild, and H3 (much simpler, no prediction) is the better candidate.
- In Phase 2, H3's exposure rule is also the planned *position-sizing* layer for any strategy that passes.

## Rationale

- **Evidence:** volatility clusters: calm periods follow calm periods, turbulent ones follow turbulent ones. Scaling
  exposure by recent volatility has improved risk-adjusted returns in equities ("volatility-managed portfolios",
  Moreira & Muir, 2017).
- **Mechanism:** high-volatility stretches in crypto often coincide with crashes, so holding less then should cut
  drawdowns more than it cuts returns.
- **Honest caveats:**
  - I don't know of solid crypto-specific evidence for this, so treat it as unproven here.
  - Volatility spikes *after* a crash starts, so the first leg down is fully taken.
  - In strong bull markets with high volatility (e.g. 2020–21), it will hold less and underperform buy & hold.

## Costs

0.30% per side (base), **0.60% (pre-screen)**, 0.90% (informational). Applied to each partial rebalance's notional.

## Expected trades / year

Roughly **10–30 partial rebalances per sleeve**, each moving ≥ 10% of the sleeve. Turnover will be reported, since
it, not the trade count, drives costs here.

## Success criteria (pre-screen)

The pass rule in conventions §7, against each asset's buy & hold and against 50/50 buy & hold, **with one
replacement**. H3 never exits fully, so its drawdown can't be expected to reach 40% or less if buy & hold fell about
80%. Criterion 2 becomes: max drawdown at least **25% smaller** (in relative terms) than the benchmark's.

> Approved at registration: a deliberate, pre-stated softening for an always-invested strategy. Held to the same
> ≤ 40% bar, H3 would very likely fail and then serve only as a benchmark.

## Kill criteria

- Fails the (adapted) pass rule.
- Turnover above 400% of capital per year at the selected variant (costs would dominate live trading at €100).

## Max variants

12 (the grid above).

## Feasibility notes (not part of the test)

- **Minimum order size bites here.** A 10-percentage-point change on a €50 sleeve is a **€5** trade: exactly Bitvavo's
  minimum. Anything smaller can't be executed at €100 capital. The fixed 10-point band was chosen for this reason, not
  from data.
- If H3 is later used as a sizing layer for H1, the same constraint applies.
