# H6 — Funding-rate crowding filter, BTC/EUR + ETH/EUR

Status: **REGISTERED**. Date drafted: 2026-10-25. Date registered: 2026-10-25 (approved as written, with the
50% base exposure), before any funding-rate data was downloaded.
Plan: PROJECT_PLAN.md amendment A3. Shared rules: [_CONVENTIONS.md](_CONVENTIONS.md), including amendment 1.

## What has and hasn't been seen

- **No funding-rate data has been downloaded or looked at.** The only check was a listing of Binance's archive file
  names: monthly files for BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT exist from **2020-01 to 2026-09**.
- No strategy result of any kind has been computed with a funding signal.
- **Public knowledge (not our data):** funding tends to run high in strong rallies, and some well-known crashes
  (for example 19 May 2021) were made worse by forced liquidation of leveraged longs. Both are pre-2025, so they're
  allowed as reasoning under A3. Nothing about 2025–2026 informed this card.

## Rule (plain words)

Trade BTC and ETH independently, each in its own sleeve with **50% of capital**. Each day, at the close:

1. **Funding signal F:** the funding rates of Binance's USDT-margined perpetual (BTCUSDT or ETHUSDT) that settled
   during the last **L** UTC days, summed per day, averaged, and annualised (× 365).
   - Only settlements strictly before the daily close count. The one at exactly 00:00 belongs to the next day.
   - Summing per day keeps the signal correct if Binance changes the funding interval.
2. **Crowded state:** turns on when **F ≥ θ**, and off when **F < θ − 10 points** (fixed hysteresis, not tuned).
3. **Target exposure** of the sleeve:
   - not crowded: **base exposure, fixed at 50%** of the sleeve (see decision 1);
   - crowded: **x × base**.

Orders fill at the next open (conventions §3). Trading is spot only. The perpetual is used as a signal and never
traded (PROJECT_PLAN.md §2.2 keeps perpetuals out of scope).

## Rationale (who loses to me, and why)

- **Mechanism:** perpetual futures pay funding from longs to shorts when the perpetual trades above spot. Persistently
  high funding means leveraged long demand is crowded: many traders are paying a high carry to hold leveraged
  longs. A modest price drop then forces liquidations, which push the price down further (a cascade).
- **Who's on the other side:** over-leveraged long traders. Their liquidations are forced sales at bad prices, and
  sitting out (or holding less) through crowded periods avoids buying from, or holding alongside, them.
- **Evidence:** Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov, *Crypto Carry*, BIS Working Paper 1087 (April 2023,
  https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm), on BTC and ETH: carry averages over 10% a year, can reach about 60%, and
  **high carry predicts future price crashes**. They attribute it to retail traders chasing leveraged upside and
  scarce arbitrage capital. Checked against the abstract and summary on 2026-10-25, not the full regressions.
- **Why it's outside H1–H5's family:** the signal comes from derivatives positioning, not from BTC/ETH price trend or
  realised volatility.
- **Honest expectation:**
  - Crowded periods cluster in rallies, so the filter will often sit out part of a rise. That costs return in
    bull markets, and the Calmar test against buy & hold may be hard.
  - It does nothing in slow bear markets with low funding (2022, public history). Drawdown control there comes only
    from the fixed 50% base exposure.
  - A fail is a realistic outcome.

## Data / period

- **Signal:** Binance USD-M funding-rate archives (`data.binance.vision`, `futures/um/monthly/fundingRate`),
  BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT. New ingest with checksum checks, validation and a manifest, as in Phase 1 (A3 step 2). The
  holdout part (2025-01 → 2026-09) is downloaded and quality-checked only.
- **Prices:** Kraken BTC/EUR and ETH/EUR, 1d (primary), as in the conventions.
- **Development: 2020-02-01 → 2024-12-31.** The archive starts in January 2020, which warms up the longest lookback
  (14 days). This loses 2018–2019, but keeps the March 2020 crash, the 2021 bull run, the 2022 bear market and the
  2023–2024 rise.
- **Benchmarks** over the same period: 50/50 buy & hold, and each asset's buy & hold (conventions §5).

## Kill criteria

- Fails any of criteria 1–2 above.
- Fewer than **3 crowded episodes per asset** in development for the selected variant: too few to tell anything, so
  killed as untestable.
- Passes only at the corner L = 3, θ = 20%, x = 0 (the fastest, most active setting).

## Decisions for you at registration

1. **Base exposure fixed at 50%** rather than 100%. With 100%, the 40% drawdown cap would almost certainly decide the
   result on its own: buy & hold fell far more than 40% in 2022 (public history), and this filter isn't designed to
   help in slow, low-funding declines. At 50%, the Calmar test and R5 decide whether the *signal* adds anything.
   Scaling exposure doesn't change Calmar much, so it doesn't make criterion 1 easier. R5 uses the same base, so a
   pass can't come from the lower exposure alone.
2. **Binance USDT-M funding as the signal** while trading EUR spot on Bitvavo. Binance is the largest perpetual
   market, so its funding is the best available crowding gauge. It's used as public data only.
3. **Development from 2020-02-01**, because of the archive's start date.
4. The **grid values** and the **R1–R6 thresholds** above, especially R5 (the random-crowded-periods null) and R2's
   6 of 10 windows.
5. ~~Verify the BIS paper's claims (Rationale) before approving.~~ Done 2026-10-25: the abstract and summary
   support the claim as cited.

## Amendments

### Amendment 1 — Details the card leaves open, fixed before the first run (2026-10-25)

Status: **REGISTERED 2026-10-25** (approved as written, with the 10-point band). Written while building the
Gate 1′ runner (`src/aitradingbot/analysis/h6.py`), before H6's rule was run on any real data. Each point copies a
precedent where one exists.

1. **Rebalance band: 10 percentage points**, as H4 and H5. A sleeve trades when its target and its current
   exposure differ by more than 10 points, and a full exit (x = 0) always trades. Without a band, the 50% base
   would be topped up or trimmed every day as prices drift. *The one material choice:* the alternative is no band
   (daily rebalancing to exactly 50%), which adds small daily trades and their costs. R5 uses the same band, so
   the null stays comparable either way.
2. **Costs of R2, R4 and R5**, which the card doesn't state: R2 and R5 at **2×** (0.60%), because conventions §4
   judge every pre-screen criterion at 2×. R4 at **1×** (0.30%), because the holdout will be judged at modelled
   costs (A3 step 5) and H5's Monte Carlo used them too.
3. **R1 rounding:** halves round up (L = 14 gives 11 and 18), as H5's G4 rounded 112.5 to 113.
4. **R6 "no sign reversal":** the Binance portfolio CAGR at 2× costs must be > 0. Kraken's must already be > 0
   (criterion 3), so this is the same Binance check H1, H4 and H5 had.
5. **Corner kill:** H6 is killed if §8 selects the corner L = 3, θ = 20%, x = 0, as H1's corner rule was applied.
6. **Episodes** (kill rule and R5) are runs of the crowded state among the decisions that execute inside the
   development window. R5's random placement keeps at least one uncrowded day between episodes so they don't merge,
   as H5's G5 did.
7. **A day without funding settlements** gives no signal update (conventions §1: gaps aren't filled). The data has
   no such day in 2020–2024 (journal #14).
