# H2 — Weekly dual-momentum rotation: BTC vs ETH vs EUR cash

Status: **REGISTERED**. Date drafted: 2026-10-19. Date registered: 2026-10-19 (approved as written).
Shared rules: [_CONVENTIONS.md](_CONVENTIONS.md) (data, timing, costs, metrics, pass rule, variant selection).

## Rule (plain words)

Once a week, at the **Sunday daily close (UTC)**, measure each asset's return over the last L days:
1. **Absolute momentum:** if neither BTC nor ETH has a positive L-day return, hold **100% EUR**.
2. **Relative momentum:** otherwise, hold **100% of whichever has the higher L-day return** (if it's positive).
3. **Switching buffer s:** only switch from the asset you hold to the other one if its L-day return is higher by more
   than s percentage points. Moving to or from cash because of rule 1 is never buffered.

Orders fill at Monday's open. All capital is in at most one asset, so this is more concentrated than H1.

## Rationale (who loses to me, and why)

- **Evidence:** "dual momentum" combines time-series momentum (as in H1) with relative momentum, i.e. owning whatever
  has been strongest recently (Antonacci's dual momentum; for crypto's relative momentum, Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu,
  2022).
- **Mechanism:** capital rotates between BTC and ETH in waves ("alt seasons"), and those waves persist for weeks to
  months.
- **Who's on the other side:** holders who rebalance back to fixed weights, and traders buying the laggard.
- **Honest expectation:** with only **two** assets, the relative-momentum part is thin and probably noisy. Much of any
  benefit will come from the cash filter, which overlaps H1. So H2 is mainly a check on whether *choosing between*
  BTC and ETH adds anything beyond H1's in-or-out decision. Weekly checks also mean reacting up to 7 days late to
  a crash.

## Parameters (2) and ranges

| Parameter | Values | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Lookback L (days) | 30, 60, 90, 120, 180 | 5 |
| Switching buffer s | 0, 5, 10 percentage points | 3 |

**15 variants** (cap 20). Rebalance day (Sunday close) and the absolute-momentum threshold (0%) are fixed, not
tuned.

## Data / period

Primary: Kraken BTC/EUR and ETH/EUR, 1d, development 2018-01-01 → 2022-12-31 (warm-up from earlier data).
Confirmation series as in the conventions. The 4h robustness check doesn't apply (the rule is weekly); instead, as
an informational check, re-run with the rebalance on **Wednesday** close to make sure the result doesn't hinge on
the weekday.

## Costs

0.30% per side (base), **0.60% (pre-screen)**, 0.90% (informational). A BTC→ETH switch counts as **two sides**
(sell, then buy), because there is no direct BTC/ETH pair in the plan.

## Expected trades / year

Roughly **10–25 switches**: more for short L and no buffer. At 2× costs, a BTC→ETH switch costs about 1.2% of
capital, so churn is the main risk.

## Success criteria (pre-screen)

The pass rule in conventions §7 against **50/50 BTC-ETH buy & hold**, with one adaptation. H2 is a single portfolio
that rotates between the two assets, so "BTC and ETH separately" (criterion 5) can't be tested directly. Instead:
5′. H2 must also beat **H1's selected variant** on Calmar at 2× costs. If it doesn't, rotation adds nothing beyond
trend following, and H1 is simpler.

## Kill criteria

- Fails the conventions §7 pass rule, including 5′.
- More than 60% of total profit comes from a single holding period (one asset held continuously). That would be one
  lucky call, not a rule.

## Max variants

15 (the grid above), plus the one informational Wednesday re-run of the selected variant.

## Feasibility notes (not part of the test)

- Each switch trades the full balance (€100), well above the €5 minimum.
- A switch needs two orders (sell BTC→EUR, buy ETH). On Bitvavo they can fill a few seconds apart; slippage covers
  that.
